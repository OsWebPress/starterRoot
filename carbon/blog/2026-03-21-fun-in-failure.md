# Fun in Failure

> without failures there is no programming

## The idea

A group of art students from a different school reached out to some of us at Codam. They wanted to run a beginner programming workshop for highschool girls and needed people who could actually write the code. The technical direction was up to us. They brought the structure, the art focus, and the day planning. A group of bachelor art students also joined, working on the visual materials and a handout document every attendee received on the day.

When I heard about it I knew immediately I wanted in. I teach sailing on the side, so when I heard about this it was an easy yes.

We came up with the idea to build a shared canvas where participants write small code snippets to place things on it: shapes, colours, patterns, all landing in the same space at the same time. Loosely inspired by Reddit's r/place, where millions of people collaboratively painted a canvas one pixel at a time, but scaled down to something you could run in an afternoon with people who had never written a line of code before.

## The workshop

The first one was hosted at Codam Coding College with 45 highschool girls across a couple of classes. None of us had done anything like this before, which is roughly where the name comes from, there was a real chance it would fall apart. And it actually did. The students were so enthusiastic about placing things on the canvas that they hammered our server hard enough that we had to restart it mid-session. After that it was fine. They picked it up quickly, kept experimenting, and by the end the canvas was genuinely interesting to look at.

![The canvas at the end of one of the workshops](/api/images/screenshot%20amsterdam.png)

After that first success, a few of us from the Codam side ran the workshop again independently at other schools, just me and some of the students who had been involved from the start.

## What happened after

The master's art students submitted the collaboration as their final project and won a prize for it. Good for them, they earned it.

The tech team got invited to present at the 5-year anniversary of Codam Coding College. As it turned out, the Dutch queen was attending. So we ended up demoing a chaotic collaborative code canvas to royalty. Not something I had on my bingo card but I'll take it.

## The takeaway

Working with people outside of tech, especially art students, gave the project the round edges it needed to not feel like a developer project dropped on an unsuspecting classroom but actually work.
